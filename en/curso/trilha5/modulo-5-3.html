🛡️ Governance and Autonomy Levels

Governance is the set of controls that defines how AI can operate within the company—and the five levels of autonomy provide a shared language for deciding how much freedom each type of task gets.

🛡️ Define AI Governance

Governance brings together agent identity, permissions, responsibilities, auditing, logs, cost controls, approved environments, accessible data, prohibited actions, human approval, security policy, information retention, and incident handling.

🆕 New here?

Governance it isn’t bureaucracy: it’s the set of decisions that lets you say "yes" safely. Without it, each team decides on its own what AI can access — and the organization discovers its rules after the first incident. Audit is being able to answer, after the fact, who did what, when, and with what authorization.

✓ Governance that works

  • ✓Fits on one page and is read by the people who will use it
  • ✓Says what's allowed, not just what's forbidden
  • ✓It’s verifiable: the control exists in the system, not just in the text
  • ✓Has an owner and a date of last review

✗ Governance in name only

  • ✗A 40-page document no one has opened
  • ✗Generic prohibitions that the team works around
  • ✗A rule with no corresponding technical control
  • ✗Written by someone who has never used an agent

🪪 Give the agent an identity and permissions

An agent running with someone's personal credentials makes auditing and accountability impossible—and grants access that no one reviewed. The agent is a system user, and it must have its own identity, minimal scope, and audit trail.

💰 Manage cost, data, and environments

Three controls that are often remembered too late: how much you can spend, what data can enter the context, and which environments the agent can run in.

1

Capped, visible cost

Set limits per task and per team, with an alert before they’re exceeded. Track the cost per completed task — it’s the metric that supports or sinks the investment at the next budget meeting.

2

Data: what never goes into the context

Personal data, secrets, regulated information. This is a privacy and compliance issue, not a technical detail—and the response needs to be written down before anyone needs it in a hurry.

3

Authorized environments

A short, explicit list of where the agent can run. Production is excluded by default, and adding it to this list is a recorded decision, not a side effect of configuration.

🪜 Learn the five levels of autonomy

This scale is the shared language that replaces vague discussions about “whether to use AI.” The right question is: for what kind of task, at what level?

How to read: each step brings the control that supports it. You don’t "choose" level 4— you reach it once you have gates, rules, a sandbox, and auditing in place. Level 5 appears in red because it’s a limit, not a goal.

5

📈 Level up with evidence

Autonomy granted in a burst of enthusiasm is taken back at the first scare — and then the team loses even what was already working. Promote a type of task based on the numbers.

📊 What to measure before promoting

  • • Approval without correction: how many deliverables passed review without rework?
  • • Attributable incidents: how many production issues came from this workflow?
  • • Review time: did it go down or up since the agent joined?
  • • Cost per task delivered: is it stable, going up, or going down?
  • • Correct stop rate: when it didn't know, did it stop and ask?

💡 Practical tip

Promote in a limited pilot: one repository, one task type, for one month, with metrics agreed on before starting. And make it clear that moving down a level is a normal decision—not a failure of the team or the technology.

🧾 Build the governance checklist

One page that answers seven questions. It’s the artifact that lets you bring AI use to other teams without repeating the entire discussion every time.

🧪 Copyable exercise — your team’s checklist

Objective: move beyond theory with a document ready to share. Fill it out and take it to the person responsible for the system.

# Governança de agentes — <time / sistema>
Dono: <nome> · Última revisão: <data>

1. QUEM é o agente?      <identidade, onde as credenciais vivem>
2. O QUE pode acessar?   <repos, ambientes, dados — e o que NUNCA entra no contexto>
3. O QUE faz sozinho?    <lista curta: tarefas reversíveis e verificáveis>
4. O QUE exige aprovação?<deploy, migração, secrets, infra, dados de produção>
5. QUANTO pode gastar?   <teto por tarefa e por mês; quem é avisado no estouro>
6. O QUE é registrado?   <ações, comandos, custo, aprovações — e por quanto tempo>
7. QUEM responde?        <pela mudança entregue e pelo incidente causado>

Nível de autonomia atual, por tipo de tarefa:
- correção de CI ......... nível <N>
- feature pequena ........ nível <N>
- migração de banco ...... nível <N>

How to verify: show the page to someone outside the team and ask them to answer: "can the agent modify the production database?" If they can’t find the answer in thirty seconds, the document isn’t ready yet.
